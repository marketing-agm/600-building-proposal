The Assignment

|Plaza 600 · 600 Stewart Street

Plaza 600 is a twenty-story, 217,322-square-foot Class A office tower in Seattle’s Denny Triangle, built in 1969 and repositioned through approximately $16.8 million of capital investment since 2020. The asset carries a diversified roster of office tenants, ground-floor retail and two levels of subterranean parking, with substantial vacancy to be leased and a building plant of 1969 vintage to be operated. This proposal is scoped to those requirements.

Capital Program in Context

|Completed & Outstanding

Approximately $16.8 million, or about $77 per square foot, has been invested in the asset since 2020, across the lobby reposition, a chiller replacement, standpipe installation, building automation controls, corridor and restroom upgrades, an accessibility lift, a cooling tower rebuild, parking and security equipment, and tenant improvement capital. That program materially reduces near-term base building requirements without eliminating them. AGM’s first capital deliverable is to separate completed work from work still outstanding.

  • Completed Capital Verification: Each project line is reconciled against invoices, permits, closeout documents and warranties, so that Ownership holds evidence of the work delivered and of what remains under warranty.
  • Remaining Base Building Scope: Chilled-water plant remaining life, elevator modernization horizon, curtain wall and sealant condition, roof membrane remaining life, riser and standpipe completion, and electrical capacity for planned tenant loads. Each item is scoped, priced and sequenced.
  • Tenant Improvement Delivery: With substantial vacancy to absorb, tenant improvement and spec suite delivery is the dominant capital activity of the hold period. AGM manages it as a construction program with defined budgets, schedules and rent commencement dates.
  • Five-Year Capital Plan: A single forward plan stating annual cost, priority and the reserve required, reviewed with Ownership at budget and updated as condition assessments and system performance data accumulate.
  • Reserve Adequacy: The capital reserve is tested against the plan so that the schedule is funded on the timeline it assumes.
Deliverable: A deferred maintenance and capital schedule priced and sequenced by year, built from the equipment record, the recurring task list, and any third-party condition assessment obtained at acquisition

Plaza-Level Retail Operations

|Mixed-Use Coordination

Ground-floor retail operates on different hours, service requirements and lease economics than the office floors above it. AGM administers the operational interface between the two uses.

  • Operating Interface: Hours of operation, plaza and lobby access, delivery routing, waste and grease handling, and venting or exhaust obligations administered against the retail lease and building rules.
  • Shared Systems: Responsibility is allocated for shared mechanical, plumbing, electrical and life-safety systems serving both retail and office use, so that repair responsibility is established in advance of any event.
  • Lease Economics: Triple-net retail administration alongside full-service office leases, with separate recovery mechanics, separate reconciliation, and sales reporting and percentage rent collection where the lease provides for it.
  • Tenant Mix: Retail positioned as an amenity to the office roster and to plaza traffic, with vacant retail merchandised toward uses that serve the building’s daytime population.
  • Presentation: Storefront, signage and plaza condition are maintained to the standard of the repositioned lobby. The plaza level is the point of arrival for prospective office tenants.

Leasing is executed by the brokerage team Ownership appoints. AGM’s role is to support that team by maintaining the building in showing condition, supplying accurate and current information to prospective tenants, and delivering build-outs on the schedule each lease commits to. With substantial vacancy to absorb, this support is a primary management deliverable.

Expense Recovery Administration

|Where the Reporting Complexity Sits

Plaza 600 does not operate under a single recovery structure. Office tenants are predominantly full-service gross, reimbursing their pro-rata share of expense increases over an individually negotiated base year. Retail operates on a triple-net or modified basis with its own commencement trigger. Some tenants reimburse amortized capital expenditures, and a prepaid local improvement district assessment is recovered from tenants over a multi-year period. Each is a separate calculation and each is a common source of under-billed recoveries.

Placement Considerations for High-Rise Office

|Plaza 600

A twenty-story tower of this vintage places differently than the balance of a portfolio. Earthquake limits and deductibles, the schedule of values, boiler and machinery coverage for a central plant, and business interruption assumptions on a partially leased building each require a defined position before binding. AGM reviews these with Ownership and the broker in advance of each renewal.

  • Schedule of Values: Insured square footage and total insured value are reconciled against rentable area and the replacement cost basis, so that the schedule matches the asset and the premium is calculated on accurate figures.
  • Earthquake: Limits and deductibles are set against lender requirements and Ownership’s risk tolerance. This is a material line item for a Seattle high-rise, with significant variance between the available options.
  • Equipment Breakdown: Boiler and machinery coverage is confirmed against the central chilled-water plant, district steam interface, elevators and emergency generator.
  • Business Interruption: Rental value and business interruption assumptions are based on in-place income and the lease-up plan rather than stabilized projections.
  • Tenant and Vendor Coverage: Tenant and vendor certificates are verified against lease and contract requirements, with the landlord named as required and limits appropriate to the work performed.
  • Claims Administration: Incidents are documented from the operating record, claims are filed and tracked to resolution, and loss history is maintained to support future placement.
Program Conditions: Availability, pricing, and coverage terms remain subject to underwriting review, property eligibility, and carrier approval

Management transitions carry recurring points of failure: a lapsed maintenance interval, an unfavorable automatic renewal, an expired certificate, an unreconciled receivable. AGM administers transition as a scheduled program with named deliverables at fixed intervals. On this asset, a substantial portion of the underlying work is already complete.
