The Assignment

|Plaza 600 · 600 Stewart Street

Plaza 600 is a twenty-story, 217,322-square-foot Class A office tower in Seattle’s Denny Triangle, built in 1969 and repositioned through approximately $16.8 million of capital investment since 2020. The asset carries a diversified roster of seventeen office tenants, ground-floor retail, and two levels of subterranean parking — with substantial vacancy to be leased and a 1969 building plant to be operated. This proposal is scoped to that combination.

Capital Program in Context

|Completed & Outstanding

Approximately $16.8 million — about $77 per square foot — has been invested in the asset since 2020, across the lobby reposition, a chiller replacement, standpipe installation, building automation controls, corridor and restroom upgrades, an accessibility lift, a cooling tower rebuild, parking and security equipment, and tenant improvement capital. That program materially reduces near-term base building requirements. It does not eliminate them, and AGM’s first capital deliverable is to separate what has been completed from what remains.

  • Completed Capital Verification: Each project line reconciled against invoices, permits, closeout documents, and warranties, so Ownership holds evidence of what was actually delivered and what remains under warranty.
  • Remaining Base Building Scope: Chilled-water plant remaining life, elevator modernization horizon, curtain wall and sealant condition, roof membrane remaining life, riser and standpipe completion, and electrical capacity for planned tenant loads — each scoped, priced, and sequenced rather than deferred.
  • Tenant Improvement Delivery: With substantial vacancy to absorb, tenant improvement and spec suite delivery is the dominant capital activity of the hold period; AGM manages it as a construction program with budgets, schedules, and rent commencement dates that carry consequences.
  • Five-Year Capital Plan: A single forward plan with annual cost, priority, and the reserve it requires — reviewed with Ownership at budget and updated as condition assessments and system performance data accumulate.
  • Reserve Adequacy: The capital reserve tested against the plan rather than set by convention, so the schedule is funded on the timeline it assumes.
Deliverable: A deferred maintenance and capital schedule priced and sequenced by year, built from the equipment record, the recurring task list, and any third-party condition assessment obtained at acquisition

Plaza-Level Retail Operations

|Mixed-Use Coordination

Ground-floor retail runs on different hours, service needs and lease economics than the office floors above it. AGM manages that interface deliberately.

  • Operating Interface: Hours of operation, plaza and lobby access, delivery routing, waste and grease handling, and venting or exhaust obligations administered against the retail lease and building rules.
  • Shared Systems: Clear allocation of responsibility for shared mechanical, plumbing, electrical, and life-safety systems serving both retail and office use, so repair responsibility is settled before an event rather than after it.
  • Lease Economics: Triple-net retail administration alongside full-service office leases — separate recovery mechanics, separate reconciliation, and sales reporting and percentage rent collection where the lease provides for it.
  • Tenant Mix: Retail positioned as an amenity to the office roster and to plaza traffic, with vacant retail merchandised toward uses that serve the building’s daytime population.
  • Presentation: Storefront, signage, and plaza condition maintained to the standard of the repositioned lobby, since the plaza level is the first thing every prospective office tenant sees.

Leasing is executed by the brokerage team Ownership appoints. AGM’s role is to make their deals close faster — a building that shows well, accurate information in every prospect’s hands, and build-outs delivered on the schedule the lease commits to. With substantial vacancy to absorb, that is a primary management deliverable, not an ancillary one.

Expense Recovery Administration

|Where the Reporting Complexity Sits

Plaza 600 does not have a single recovery structure. Office tenants are predominantly full-service gross, reimbursing their pro-rata share of expense increases over an individually negotiated base year; retail operates on a triple-net or modified basis with its own commencement trigger; some tenants reimburse amortized capital expenditures; and a prepaid local improvement district assessment is recovered from tenants over a multi-year period. Each of these is a separate calculation, and each one is a place where recoveries are commonly under-billed.

Placement Considerations — High-Rise Office

|Plaza 600

A twenty-story Seattle tower of this vintage places differently than the balance of a portfolio. Earthquake limits and deductibles, the schedule of values, boiler and machinery coverage for a central plant, and business interruption assumptions on a partially leased building all require deliberate positions before binding. AGM works these with Ownership and the broker rather than accepting a renewal as issued.

  • Schedule of Values: Insured square footage and total insured value reconciled against rentable area and the replacement cost basis, so the schedule matches the asset and the premium is not built on a mismatch.
  • Earthquake: Limits and deductibles set deliberately against lender requirements and Ownership’s risk tolerance — a material line item for a Seattle high-rise and one where the difference between options is significant.
  • Equipment Breakdown: Boiler and machinery coverage confirmed against the central chilled-water plant, district steam interface, elevators, and emergency generator.
  • Business Interruption: Rental value and business interruption assumptions built on actual in-place income and the lease-up plan, not on stabilized projections.
  • Tenant & Vendor Coverage: Tenant and vendor certificates verified against lease and contract requirements, with the landlord named as required and limits appropriate to the work performed.
  • Claims Administration: Incident documentation from the operating record, claims filed and tracked to resolution, and loss history maintained to support future placement.
Program Conditions: Availability, pricing, and coverage terms remain subject to underwriting review, property eligibility, and carrier approval

Transitions fail in predictable ways: a maintenance interval lapses, a contract auto-renews on bad terms, a certificate expires unnoticed, a receivable goes unreconciled. AGM treats transition as a scheduled program with named deliverables — and on this asset much of the underlying work is already complete.
